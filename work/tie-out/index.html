TIE-OUT

Chart-of-accounts mapping for roll-ups, and a close pack the system will refuse to publish if it cannot stand behind it.

Stack Python · DuckDB · dbt · Fellegi-Sunter record linkage Size ~3,200 lines in tieout/ · 12 modules Tests 124 passing in 47.95s (run 2026-09-09) Repo private

A roll-up buys companies, and each one arrives with its own chart of accounts. Consolidation forces those charts onto one group chart, and that mapping is where a close pack goes wrong quietly. The interesting failure is not the one that breaks a total. It is the one that reconciles to the cent and is still wrong.

1 · The problem: two charts that disagree, one consolidated number

Every acquired entity keeps its own account numbers, its own account names, and its own view of what belongs in cost of sales. None of that is an error. It is what a chart of accounts is for. Consolidation still has to produce one set of captions, so somebody decides that this entity's account 5000 belongs under that group account, account by account, entity by entity, every period.

The checks that usually guard this are conservation (every source dollar lands somewhere in the group) and sign preservation (nothing flipped across the ledger). Both are necessary. Neither looks at meaning. TIE-OUT exists because the gap between those two facts is large enough to hold a six-figure misstatement, and it makes that claim in a form that can be run rather than argued.

2 · The claim it exists to disprove

If every source dollar lands in the group and nothing crossed the ledger, the mapping is correct.

That claim is false, and the disproof ships as a passing test rather than a paragraph. scripts/prove_tests_fire.py injects one permutation into a clean run: entity E1 account 5000, COGS - Materials, mapped to group account G-6100, Rent and Occupancy. Then it asserts which checks must pass and which single check must fail. The script name is the point: the tests are themselves under test, because a check that cannot be shown to fire is not evidence.

Three periods, three wrong subtotals, and a trial balance that reconciles in all three. The two checks a reviewer would reach for first report green, correctly, because the thing they measure really did not change.

3 · Why it hides, and why the oracle catches it

Why it hides

Both accounts are debit-normal, so no sign flips. The row is assigned exactly once, so no dollar is duplicated or dropped. Every arithmetic invariant a consolidation normally enforces is satisfied, and the entity still ties to the cent.

What changed is which caption the dollars report under, and gross profit is the first subtotal that notices. A tighter tolerance cannot find this, because the error is exactly zero in every dimension a tolerance measures.

Why the oracle catches it

The oracle compares reconstructed captions against subtotals the entity itself published from its own chart. That classification was made before any group chart existed, and the mapper never reads it.

So the check has an input the thing it is checking cannot influence. That is an independent oracle, not a stricter version of the checks that already passed. It is also the design idea I would carry into any other consolidation problem: find the number somebody already published for their own reasons, and make it the referee.

4 · The harness, and what it says about itself

The disproof shows the system catching a planted error. The harness is the part I would open first in an interview, because it is where the system is least flattering to itself. It reports four things, and two of them are arguments against its own numbers.

Section 1: silence is the correct output

Eight accounts are renamed to synonyms the rules do not cover. The system stays silent on all eight and is confidently wrong on none. Coverage was never the target. The target is that unfamiliar vocabulary produces a question and never a guess, because a guess at this layer arrives downstream wearing the same confidence as a rule.

Section 2: the review burden collapses, 21 questions to 0

In 2026-05 the system asks about all 21 accounts and maps none automatically. In 2026-06, the same 21 accounts, it asks nothing and maps all 21. In 2026-07 there are 23 accounts: 21 already carry an approved decision, so 2 questions.

That is the whole economic argument for the tool, and it is a count of human interactions rather than a score. A judgment a controller makes once is not asked again, and the questions that survive are the ones that are genuinely new. It also means the review queue is a leading indicator: if it stops shrinking across periods, something upstream changed.

Sections 4 and 5: where the system argues against its own numbers

Name similarity scores a ranking AUC of 0.727 across the full cross-product, which is above chance and is the number a paper would report. At the operating point where the feature would actually run, top-1 accuracy is 30/51, or 58.8%, and of the 21 top-1 errors, 8 cross the balance-sheet and P&L line. The harness prints its own conclusion: AUC says the feature is fine; top-1 says it is unusable as a selector. The code agrees with the harness. Similarity is barred from may_propose_destination, so it can rank candidates for a human and cannot choose one. Ranking and selection are different jobs, and a metric chosen for the first will happily license the second.

Section 5 is the number that would look best on a slide, which is why the harness prints a warning directly under it. Agreement with the answer key is E1 23/23, E2 14/14, E3 17/17, and the system labels it CIRCULAR in its own report, because the charts, the rules and the key all came from the same author. Its words: this number measures my consistency with myself. It is not evidence of correctness and must not be quoted as an accuracy figure. It is not quoted as one here, and it should not be quoted as one anywhere.

5 · Design decisions

Three choices that took the longest to settle. Each exists because the obvious version fails quietly, which is the only failure mode this system treats as unacceptable.

DecisionWhat the code does Why
Mapping confidence An ordinal provenance label, not a score. The enum raises on +, * and float(). Confidence here records how a mapping was decided, so the average of two provenances is not a weaker claim, it is a meaningless one. Making the arithmetic unrepresentable is cheaper than policing every place a mean could be taken.
Money Integer minor units stored in BIGINT. DuckDB silently drops to binary float on any division. Every check on this page is an equality to the cent, and float turns that into approximate equality. Once a system needs a tolerance, it can no longer tell a representation artefact from a real difference, which is exactly the distinction the oracle depends on.
Normal balance A property of the account, not of the element it rolls up to. Contra accounts break element-derived inference. Deriving the sign from the element is right often enough to pass a demo and wrong on precisely the accounts that exist to carry the opposite sign.

6 · What I would do differently, and what is next

Get charts I did not write. Section 5 already says the honest limit out loud: I authored the charts, the rules and the key, so agreement between them measures consistency and nothing else. The next real test is a chart of accounts from a source I have never seen, mapped against a close pack somebody else signed. Until that run exists, no number here should be read as an accuracy figure, and the harness says so before a reader has to.

Widen the held-out vocabulary. Eight renamed accounts is a smoke test for the escalation path, not a generalisation claim. It proves the system stays silent when it should. It does not yet say how often it should have stayed silent and did not.

Leave similarity barred until top-1 earns the release. The feature is not going to be unblocked by a better AUC, because AUC was never the thing in doubt. It gets unblocked when top-1 at the operating point is good enough that a wrong first suggestion costs a reviewer less than no suggestion, and when errors stop crossing the balance-sheet and P&L line.

What I would do earlier next time. The independent oracle I would build the same way on day one. The harness I would build sooner. Labelling a number CIRCULAR on the day it first prints costs nothing; an unlabelled number gets quoted, and then it has to be withdrawn from a room that already believed it.
