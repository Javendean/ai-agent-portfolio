The project: TIE-OUT

TIE-OUT — chart-of-accounts mapping for roll-ups

Python · DuckDB · dbt · Fellegi-Sunter record linkage · 124 tests

Maps the charts of accounts of acquired companies onto a group chart, and refuses to publish a close pack it cannot stand behind. The interesting part is not the mapping — it is what the system does when it is unsure, and how it tells apart the numbers agree from the mapping is right.

The claim it exists to disprove: "If every source dollar lands in the group and nothing crossed the ledger, the mapping is correct." It ships as a passing test, not a paragraph. Map COGS - Materials to Rent and Occupancy:

✓ PASSES — sign check. Both accounts are debit-normal.
✓ PASSES — value conservation. The row is assigned exactly once.
✓ PASSES — the company's books tie to the cent.
✗ Gross profit is wrong by $309,245.88

Only the third check catches it, and only because it compares the reconstructed captions against the subtotals the entity itself published from its own chart — a classification its bookkeeper made before any group chart existed, which the mapper never reads. That is an independent oracle, not a tighter tolerance.

Design decisions worth the interview: mapping confidence is an ordinal provenance label, not a score — the enum actively raises on +, * and float() so averaging two mappings is unrepresentable rather than merely discouraged. Money is integer minor units in BIGINT, because DuckDB silently drops to binary float on any division. Normal balance is a property of the account, not the element — contra accounts break element-derived inference.
