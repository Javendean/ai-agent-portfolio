Analyst and engineer. I build the systems that decide when a model's output is trustworthy enough to write into a system of record.

Work

TIE-OUT

Maps an acquired company's chart of accounts onto the group chart, and refuses to publish a close pack it cannot stand behind.

Python · DuckDB · dbt · Fellegi-Sunter record linkage · 124 tests in 47.95s

Its evaluation harness argues against its own numbers: name similarity is barred from choosing a destination at 58.8% top-1, and the flattering agreement-with-key figure is printed under the word CIRCULAR.

Verity

Turns unstructured sales notes into schema-validated CRM records. Failures become specific repair instructions and a bounded retry, never a silent success.

Python 3.12 · FastAPI · Pydantic 2 · Docker · Kubernetes · HubSpot API v3 · 82 tests in 0.54s

On messy input, every working model in a six-model benchmark scored 0% first-attempt and 100% final. That gap is what the correction loop buys.

18 row-level-security policies on 7 tables. The same message is 1 SMS segment in English and 3 in Spanish: accents and ¿/¡ force UCS-2, cutting the segment budget from 153 characters to 67.

Selected evidence

The claim worth disproving: if every source dollar lands in the group and nothing crossed the ledger, the mapping is correct. TIE-OUT ships the disproof as a passing test, not a paragraph.

Only the subtotal oracle catches it, because it compares reconstructed captions against subtotals the entity itself published from its own chart, a classification made before any group chart existed, which the mapper never reads. That is an independent oracle, not a tighter tolerance. The harness, the design decisions and the limits are in the TIE-OUT case study.
