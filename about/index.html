Value at Risk is not a valuation. It is a claim about how far an estimate can move at a stated confidence over a stated horizon, and it is only ever as good as the assumptions sitting under it, which is why its failures are quiet ones. Structured credit sharpens that: a CLO or ABS position can look orderly at the portfolio level while the loss that decides solvency sits in a tail nobody priced. Spending a capstone there is where I stopped reading a confidence number as a summary and started reading it as a claim that has to survive on its own. TIE-OUT carrying mapping confidence as an ordinal provenance label rather than a score is a direct descendant of that.

How I work

Four principles, each one taken from a decision that is visible in the work on this site rather than from a preference held in the abstract.

Make the invalid state unrepresentable

In TIE-OUT, mapping confidence is an ordinal provenance label rather than a score: the enum raises on +, * and float(), so averaging two mappings is not something you can write down. Money is integer minor units in BIGINT because DuckDB silently drops to binary float on any division. Neither rule is a validation step that somebody can forget to call.

Escalate uncertainty instead of guessing

Verity carries confidence, requires_human_review, review_reasons, ambiguity_note and amount_is_estimate, and when its correction loop is exhausted it returns the best result it has with explicit error flags rather than a silent failure. In the captured run, four fields came back flagged for a human. TIE-OUT was given 8 accounts renamed to synonyms its rules do not cover: it stayed silent on 8 and was confidently wrong on 0.

Lock the finding behind a test so it cannot come back

Measured live in production on 2026-09-09, the same LCC message costs 1 segment in English (116 characters, GSM_7BIT_EXT) and 3 in Spanish (169 characters, UTF16), because accented characters and ¿/¡ force UCS-2 and cut the per-segment budget from 153 characters to 67. One live template was doing it. Finding it was the small part; the fix is now locked behind an automated test asserting a segment budget per template, so it cannot come back quietly.

Measure, then say what the measurement does not cover

TIE-OUT agrees with its own answer key on 23/23, 14/14 and 17/17, and labels that result CIRCULAR in its own output: it measures consistency with itself, and must never be quoted as an accuracy figure. Verity's benchmark points the same discipline outward. Across 6 models × 9 trials (run 2026-03-01), every working model scored 0% first-attempt on messy input and 100% final, and llama4-scout matched llama4-maverick at about 1/5.9 the cost per success ($0.000365 against $0.002148).
